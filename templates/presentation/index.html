Designing around attention and capacity—not the endless maintenance of a backlog.

01 · The tension

A list is not a plan

Most tools record everything. Very few help someone decide what deserves their finite week.

“By Wednesday, my plan is just another place reminding me what I haven’t done.”Weekly planning interview · participant 04

02 · What people need

Three signals, at a glance

A useful plan makes tradeoffs legible before work begins.

1
MeaningThe few outcomes that would make the week count.
2
CapacityThe time already claimed by meetings and commitments.
3
PermissionWhat can be left alone without creating quiet anxiety.

03 · The first release

One week. Three outcomes.

Start with a constraint that turns prioritization into a visible, reversible act.

Name the week

Choose the theme before choosing tasks.

Place three outcomes

Fit them around real capacity, not ideal capacity.

Close the loop

Reflect on Friday and carry forward only deliberately.

04 · What success feels like

Relief is the metric

We will learn from behavior, but the product promise is emotional: less ambiguity at the start and less residue at the end.
