# Field note: make the week feel lighter

*A short product brief · August 2026*

> A useful planning system should reduce the number of decisions people carry in their heads—not add another place to manage.

## The observation

People rarely struggle because they cannot list their work. They struggle because a list does not explain **what matters now**, what can wait, or when they have taken on too much.

Our opportunity is a weekly planning view that begins with capacity and intent. Instead of asking people to continuously groom a backlog, it asks three humane questions:

1. What would make this week feel meaningfully complete?
2. What time is already spoken for?
3. What can we deliberately leave alone?

## A narrow first release

The first version should do less, unusually well.

| Moment | What the experience should do |
| --- | --- |
| Monday morning | Turn a long backlog into three clear outcomes |
| Midweek | Show whether the plan still fits without creating guilt |
| Friday afternoon | Capture what changed and carry only the right work forward |

The owner remains in control: suggestions are visible, reversible, and easy to edit. Automation can prepare a plan, but it never silently rearranges someone’s commitments.

## What we will learn

- Can someone make a credible weekly plan in under five minutes?
- Do they return to it when priorities change?
- Does Friday feel like a conclusion rather than an unfinished Monday?

## Decision

Prototype the Monday flow first. Use real upcoming commitments, keep the plan to one screen, and test whether the **three-outcome constraint** creates relief or frustration.
